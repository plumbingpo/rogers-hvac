Upfront Pricing

Clear Pricing Before Any Work Starts

Effective HVAC service starts with knowing exactly what you're dealing with. Rogers HVAC offers honest, upfront pricing throughout Rogers, handling everything from a worn capacitor to a full system replacement. Every job begins with a thorough assessment of your equipment, including its age, tonnage, fuel type, refrigerant charge, and duct condition, followed by a clear recommendation and a written price before any work starts.

The trouble with HVAC failures is that many of them develop out of sight. A pinhole leak in the evaporator coil slowly drains refrigerant, the blower bearings wear down gradually, and a weak capacitor holds on until the first hot afternoon, when the compressor won't start. When bills climb, airflow drops, or ice forms on the lines, have the system checked before a repair turns into a replacement.

Not every visit is about a breakdown. Many Rogers homeowners ask us to size up an aging system before it fails, and it's a smart move. With a typical AC or furnace lifespan of 12 to 18 years, replacing on your own schedule beats scrambling for a new unit during a cold snap. We'll give you a straight answer on how much life your equipment has left.

Maintenance Plans

Tune-Ups That Keep Your System Running

Each maintenance visit from Rogers HVAC is more than a filter change. The technician cleans the coils, verifies the refrigerant charge, tests capacitors and contactors, inspects the burners and heat exchanger, clears the condensate drain, puts in a fresh filter, and measures static pressure across the ducts. Small problems get caught while they're still cheap to fix, and the equipment stays running clean and efficient.

Every maintenance plan we write is a little different. An older furnace needs a closer look than a brand-new heat pump, and a dusty neighborhood means condenser coils clog faster. We set up spring cooling and fall heating visits around your equipment and climate, so every tune-up keeps the system efficient and extends the life of the equipment you've already paid for.

The savings show up faster than most people expect. Clean coils move heat more efficiently, a correct refrigerant charge keeps the compressor from overworking, and a fresh filter spares the blower motor. Next to the cost of replacing a system even one year early, a maintenance plan is money well spent.

Proper Sizing

The Right Size System for Your Home

Proper sizing matters more than most homeowners realize. Before quoting new equipment, Rogers HVAC works through the details that determine your home's heating and cooling load: square footage, insulation, window exposure, and duct layout. That gives you a system that runs in long, efficient cycles, keeps humidity in check, and holds every room at the temperature you set.

We walk you through the options for every home, especially growing families with rising comfort demands. A single-stage system that served a small household rarely satisfies a finished basement and two added bedrooms, and a modern two-stage or variable-speed heat pump can hold tighter temperatures while cutting energy use dramatically. If you're weighing furnace-and-AC against a heat pump, or ducted against ductless, our technician will lay out the honest trade-offs: SEER2 efficiency ratings, operating cost, electrical and venting requirements, and upfront installation work, so you can pick what fits your home and budget.

Repairs We Handle

The HVAC Repairs Rogers Homes Need Most

Homes in Rogers run into a familiar set of heating and cooling failures, and each one calls for a different fix. Rogers HVAC handles them all: no cooling, air that blows but never gets cold, furnaces that won't ignite or short-cycle, frozen evaporator coils, refrigerant leaks, condensers that hum without starting, thermostats that lose their settings, clogged condensate drains, and mini-splits flashing error codes. Our technicians identify the exact fault and target the repair to the component that actually failed.

The correct fix depends entirely on what actually failed. On cooling systems, it's usually a capacitor, contactor, or fan motor. On furnaces, it tends to be an igniter, flame sensor, or control board. Leaking ductwork pushing conditioned air into the attic is sealed and repaired, and refrigerant is recovered and recharged once the leak itself is repaired. Some of these issues are just inconvenient, but a cracked heat exchanger or a clogged condensate drain can be a real safety or water-damage risk, so we treat it with urgency.
